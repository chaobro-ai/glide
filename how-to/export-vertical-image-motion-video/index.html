Export guide

How to export a vertical image motion video.

IMAGES OF YOURS9:16 FRAMEPORTRAIT-READY MOTION

A vertical frame changes the composition, not just the dimensions. What feels balanced in a widescreen scene can feel crowded, cropped, or too distant in 9:16. Set the destination first, then choose media and movement with the portrait frame already visible.

Start with the 9:16 frame.

In the editor’s Frame controls, select 9:16 before you add keyframes or settle on a template. The preview will show the narrower vertical canvas, which makes it easier to choose images with a strong central subject, clear vertical lines, or useful foreground and background depth.

For images that were shot wide, decide what should remain visible before you animate. A close crop can be more effective than trying to keep an entire landscape inside a tall frame. A product, face, architectural detail, or path through a scene often makes a stronger vertical anchor.

Choose motion that leaves room to breathe.

  1. Choose a portrait-friendly starting composition. Focus, stack, vertical flow, and carousel templates can create a clear path through a narrow frame. Preview a few options with the same media rather than choosing by thumbnail alone.
  2. Keep the camera move narrow. A small zoom is often enough to add presence. The editor keeps the whole visible image group visually centered while zooming, so you can bring the work closer without manually correcting the composition.
  3. Let important images hold. Vertical viewers usually have less room to inspect a multi-image arrangement. Give a hero image enough time, then let the next scene make a visible shift in scale, crop, or rhythm.
  4. Preview at the intended frame. Check that captions or subject matter are not too close to an edge and that the motion feels legible at phone size, not only on a large desktop canvas.

Choose export settings deliberately.

Open Export once the motion is ready. Select MP4 or WebM, then choose the resolution and frame rate that match the intended destination and device capability. For a polished portrait master, 1080p is the practical baseline in most cases. A 30 fps export is usually sufficient for a controlled editorial sequence; use 60 fps only when the movement and destination justify the larger render.

Images of Yours uses browser-based export. When WebCodecs is available, it can render offline HD video; otherwise the editor falls back to realtime recording. Keep the editor open until the export completes.

Save the portrait version as a source project.

Save the finished edit as an .ioy file. That lets you revisit the vertical cut later, or rebuild the same material as a widescreen portfolio piece without having to recreate the scene logic from scratch.
