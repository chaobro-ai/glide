How to make a creative portfolio showcase video.

IMAGES OF YOURSFOR CREATIVE PROJECTSSTART WITH THE WORK, NOT THE EFFECT

A portfolio video is not a compressed portfolio website. It is a short, visual argument for a point of view. The useful constraint is simple: select only the frames that help someone understand the quality, range, or atmosphere of the work, then let movement create a controlled path through them.

Edit the selection before you animate it.

Choose a set of six to twelve pieces from one project or one cohesive body of work. Keep the visual density even: if the first image is a wide environmental shot, follow it with a medium frame or close detail, not another near-identical wide. If you need to introduce a complex project, use a short sequence of scenes rather than trying to explain everything in a single movement.

Place the image with the clearest point of view first. It may be a final image, a strong crop, or the detail that makes the project feel distinct. Lead with confidence rather than a title card.

Choose a composition that respects the work.

Templates should support the kind of material you are showing. Architecture and product work often benefits from grid, perspective, or focus systems that preserve lines and detail. A photography series can work beautifully with an orbit, carousel, or sequential flow. A more graphic body of work may suit a stack, wipe, or reveal.

The template library is organised by visual family so you can begin with the type of movement, not an arbitrary effect name. Preview a few with the same material. The right one is usually the option that makes the images read faster and more clearly.

Use scenes to change the argument.

  1. Open with the strongest frame. Give the viewer enough time to recognise its shape before the composition starts to expand.
  2. Use a second scene for variation. Shift from a broad overview to material detail, from a full object to a feature, or from a stable layout to a more kinetic arrangement.
  3. Guide attention with keyframes. A small zoom or tilt can bring an important image forward. Avoid moving every control at once; one clear gesture is more persuasive than continuous activity.
  4. End on the work, not the interface. Let the final visible composition feel like a considered conclusion. A still or slow final movement gives the viewer an image to remember.

Export for the place where it will be seen.

Use 16:9 for a presentation, widescreen screen, or portfolio header. Use 9:16 when the destination is a vertical social post or story. Square and 4:5 frames offer a dense, editorial format for a feed. Make the aspect decision before you fine-tune the camera because the frame changes what feels centered and how much room each image receives.

When you are ready, render a high-quality master and keep the saved .ioy project. You can always reopen the same edit for a new format without rebuilding the story from nothing.
