Workflow guide

How to create a photo motion reel in your browser.

IMAGES OF YOURS4 STEPSFOR A SMALL IMAGE COLLECTION

A good photo motion reel does not need a hundred images or a complicated edit. It needs a clear group of material, a repeatable visual rule, and a small amount of directed movement. Images of Yours lets you make that sequence with your own media in a browser-based editor.

Start with a set that belongs together.

Choose six to twelve images that share a subject, place, palette, or moment. The goal is not to show every file; it is to establish a visual rhythm. A travel story might move from a wide establishing image to close details. A product sequence might alternate hero shots with material or texture.

Before opening the editor, decide where the reel will live. A website header, portfolio, presentation, and social post all call for different frame shapes and a different first image.

Build the reel in four steps.

  1. Choose a frame and a template. In the editor, pick a landscape, square, portrait, or vertical frame. Then explore the template library. A flowing perspective template is useful for a journey; a grid or focus template gives editorial and product work a more deliberate beat.
  2. Add your images or video. Add media to the visible slots. You can change the number of slots when the selected composition needs fewer or more pieces. If you want a quick study of a style first, open the in-editor Gallery and load a curated pack.
  3. Make the movement intentional. Keep the first version simple. Add a new scene when the material needs a fresh composition, choose a transition, then use camera zoom and tilt keyframes to guide attention. The editor keeps the visible group centered while you zoom, so a camera move can feel closer without losing the composition.
  4. Preview, revise, and export. Watch for a few loops. If the reel feels busy, remove a scene or use fewer images rather than adding more effects. When the pacing works, select an export format, resolution, and frame rate, then render the final video.

Make the first second do the work.

On a scrollable feed, a reel earns attention before anyone understands the whole story. Lead with the image that contains the strongest shape, clearest face, or most distinct color. Give it a moment to register, then let the template establish the movement. The final image can echo the beginning, land on a detail, or leave an open visual question.

Choose a template by pacing, not by novelty.

For a calm series, use a composition with a stable axis: grids, stacks, and focus templates are useful starting points. For visual abundance, use an orbit, carousel, or perspective template. The point is to make the motion reinforce the work already present in your images. You can browse the template families before you start.

Once the basic reel works, save the project as an .ioy file. That gives you a reusable starting point for future versions with different media or aspect ratios.
